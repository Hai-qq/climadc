# ClimaDC v0.2 engineering replay design

Status: Phase 0 semantic contracts/local readers, the Phase 1 single-window replay kernel, the
Phase 2 Great Britain reference scenario/CLI/artifacts, the Phase 3 optional read-only adapters,
the Phase 4 rolling orchestration/upper-quantile policy, Phase 5 post-hoc marginal risk
diagnostics, and Phase 6 multi-scenario robustness suites are implemented in the development checkout. This document distinguishes those
capabilities from their explicit engineering limits.

## 1. Product question

ClimaDC v0.2 will answer one operationally meaningful question:

> Given weather, grid signals, site telemetry, and deadline-constrained batch work, how would a
> forecast-aware schedule change facility energy, emissions, cost, peak demand, and service-level
> outcomes when replayed against the values that were actually observed?

The deliverable is an offline, auditable engineering study. It is not an online controller and it
must not claim savings that were not measured by replay.

## 2. Why v0.1 is not sufficient

The v0.1 framework provides useful infrastructure: exact time semantics, leakage checks, temporal
backtesting, prediction calibration, artifact lineage, and an energy-conserving shadow optimizer.
However, the current optimizer consumes a generic prediction value as a cost index. That leaves
cooling-power forecasts, electricity prices, and carbon intensity semantically interchangeable even
though they have different units and engineering roles. The synthetic reference study verifies the
pipeline, not operational value.

v0.2 separates those roles and adds counterfactual settlement with realized signals.

## 3. Scope and non-goals

### In scope

- one site with either a complete decision window or receding-horizon orchestration that re-solves
  at each origin and commits only a configured step;
- preemptible batch work with release times, deadlines, energy requirements, and power limits;
- weather forecasts and realized weather;
- forecast and realized grid carbon intensity;
- user-supplied energy prices and optional demand-charge assumptions;
- an explicit, calibratable facility-energy model;
- baseline, forecast-aware, upper-quantile stress-scenario, and oracle schedules;
- replay using realized values and an auditable report.

### Not in scope for v0.2

- applying a schedule to production infrastructure;
- reinforcement learning;
- multi-site placement or workload migration;
- a general telemetry collector or weather model;
- a web control plane;
- claims of production savings from synthetic or simulated inputs.

## 4. Engineering model

For each interval `t`, the facility model exposes the relationship

```text
facility_power[t] = it_power[t] + cooling_power(it_power[t], weather[t], parameters)
```

The packaged reference implementation uses a documented, monotonic temperature-sensitive PUE
curve. Its parameters, provenance, and applicability limits are written into the run artifacts. A
user can provide a calibrated model through a protocol without changing the replay engine.

The current scheduler uses hard service constraints and a weighted score after each input has been
converted to its declared canonical unit:

```text
sum_t(
    interval_hours
    * facility_power_kw[t]
    * (cost_weight * energy_price_per_kwh[t]
       + carbon_weight * carbon_intensity_per_kwh[t])
)
+ cost_weight * demand_charge_per_kw * facility_peak_kw
```

When both weights are nonzero, the result is a comparison score rather than a currency amount; the
weights declare the study's trade-off between unlike quantities. Cost and emissions are reported
separately even when both appear in the optimization objective. The configured demand charge uses
the price currency and the current window peak, so it is a scenario assumption rather than a full
monthly tariff reconstruction.
Every accepted job must complete, so a deadline or capacity failure is reported as infeasible rather
than hidden behind a service-level penalty. The report must preserve the unweighted physical and
economic quantities.

## 5. Canonical v0.2 inputs

| Contract | Required meaning | Important invariants |
|---|---|---|
| `ClimateForecastFrame` | forecast weather available at decision time | existing `issue_time <= available_at <= valid_time` rule |
| `DCTelemetryFrame` | realized facility/IT/cooling measurements | explicit units and observation quality |
| `GridSignalFrame` | forecast or realized carbon intensity and energy price | forecast and realized rows are distinguished; units match the signal |
| `FlexibleWorkloadFrame` | one deferrable batch job per row | release, availability, deadline, energy, and maximum power are explicit |
| `PredictionFrame` | DC power or cooling predictions | prediction targets are never reused as price or carbon signals |

`GridSignalFrame` initially supports `carbon_intensity` and `energy_price`. Forecast rows require a
non-null issue time and satisfy `issue_time <= available_at <= valid_time`. Realized rows have no
issue time and satisfy `valid_time <= available_at`. Carbon intensity uses an emissions-per-energy
unit such as `gCO2e/kWh`; price uses a supported ISO-currency-per-energy unit such as `GBP/kWh`.

`FlexibleWorkloadFrame` initially models preemptible work. Each job has positive energy and maximum
power, a nonnegative priority, and `release_time <= available_at <= deadline`. The legacy v0.1
`WorkloadFrame` remains available until the scheduler migration is complete.

Exact columns and local-reader examples are documented in the
[engineering input guide](../concepts/engineering-inputs.md).

## 6. Decision and replay sequence

The caller supplies weather forecasts instead of invoking a forecasting model inside the replay.
The study runner can publish one origin or repeat the same causal sequence across rolling origins.
At each origin:

1. Freeze only inputs whose `available_at` is not later than the origin.
2. Predict facility power from legal weather and telemetry history.
3. Construct an ASAP baseline schedule.
4. Solve peak-only, price-only, carbon-only, and joint schedules under identical constraints; when
   configured, also solve the joint objective against a complete upper-quantile stress scenario.
5. After the horizon has elapsed, settle every schedule using realized weather, carbon intensity,
   and telemetry or the declared facility model.
6. Compare each feasible schedule with the baseline and an oracle that uses realized values.
7. Publish all assumptions, violations, forecast metrics, operational metrics, and lineage.

In rolling mode, the engine re-solves the complete horizon but commits only the configured step.
Remaining job energy is carried separately for every policy, and only committed intervals enter
aggregate settlement. An accepted job's deadline must fit inside the current complete planning
horizon; terminal carry-over beyond that horizon is not implemented. The rolling Oracle uses
realized signals but respects causal job arrivals, so its cumulative signed comparison is not a
global perfect-foresight lower bound.

The forecast objective and realized settlement must never share future information.

## 7. Required comparisons and metrics

The reference report compares:

- ASAP/no-shift baseline;
- peak-only;
- price-only;
- carbon-only;
- forecast-aware joint objective;
- upper-quantile joint objective when an exact quantile scenario is configured;
- realized-value oracle.

Required physical and service metrics are:

- facility energy (`kWh`);
- IT and cooling energy (`kWh`);
- operational emissions (`kgCO2e`);
- scenario energy cost and currency;
- facility peak (`kW`) and optional demand charge;
- shifted energy (`kWh`);
- completed jobs, deadline violations, and unserved energy;
- forecast errors and an explicit interval-coverage status; point forecasts have no coverage
  metric, while a configured upper-quantile scenario reports committed-slot marginal coverage,
  Wilson uncertainty, exceedances, and pinball loss without claiming two-sided or joint coverage;
- realized improvement versus baseline;
- regret versus oracle.

A negative result is valid. The report must not hide runs where shifting increases cost, emissions,
or peak demand.

## 8. Reference deployment

The first public scenario is a reproducible Great Britain single-site replay:

- archived weather forecasts from Open-Meteo
  [Previous Runs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/previous-runs-api) and realized weather from its
  [Historical Weather API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api);
- forecast and estimated realized carbon intensity from the official
  [NESO Carbon Intensity API](https://api.carbonintensity.org.uk/);
- a checked-in deterministic batch-workload fixture, replaceable by user CSV;
- a declared scenario tariff, replaceable by user CSV;
- a small offline fixture for CI plus an optional network refresh command.

Source timestamps, retrieval timestamps, hashes, attribution, and licenses are stored in the source
manifest. The packaged 24-hour fixture runs offline with:

```bash
climadc demo carbon-shift --output-dir ./climadc-replay-runs
```

The same prepared inputs support `climadc replay path/to/study.yaml`. A separate
`refresh-carbon-shift` command creates, but never overwrites, a network-refreshed snapshot.

## 9. Artifact changes

The existing eight benchmark artifacts remain valid. A v0.2 replay adds, at minimum:

- `assumptions.yaml` — facility model, tariff, objective weights, and units;
- `grid-signals.parquet` — legal forecast and realized settlement inputs;
- `workload.parquet` — normalized batch jobs;
- `schedules.parquet` — baseline and candidate allocations;
- `replay-metrics.json` — forecast and realized outcomes;
- `report.html` — human-readable comparisons and limitations.

Every derived number in the HTML report must be reconstructible from those artifacts.

## 10. Delivery phases

### Phase 0 — semantic spine

- add `GridSignalFrame` and `FlexibleWorkloadFrame`;
- validate causal timestamps, supported signal kinds, finite values, and physical units;
- add local CSV/Parquet readers;
- preserve v0.1 APIs and tests.

### Phase 1 — replay kernel

- implemented a replaceable facility-energy protocol and bounded temperature-sensitive PUE model;
- implemented ASAP, peak-only, price-only, carbon-only, joint, and oracle policies with linear
  capacity, release, deadline, power, and energy constraints;
- implemented demand-charge assumptions through `ReplayConfig` and normalized price signals;
- settle schedules against realized weather, price, and carbon signals;
- emit unit-aware operational metrics, explicit infeasibility, baseline changes, and oracle regret.

### Phase 2 — reference scenario

- implemented NESO and historical Open-Meteo ingestion with immutable source manifests;
- implemented the Great Britain offline fixture and optional overwrite-safe refresh command;
- implemented atomic replay artifacts and a self-contained comparative HTML report;
- implemented offline integration tests plus scheduled provider-contract checks.

### Phase 3 — optional read-only integrations

- implemented Prometheus range-matrix conversion and documented Kepler watts-gauge queries;
- implemented Carbon Aware SDK-compatible current-forecast, observed-data, and offline-payload
  conversion;
- implemented dependency-free SustainDC evaluation CSV conversion for simulator-backed telemetry
  and carbon signals.

### Phase 4 — rolling and declared risk scenario

- implemented receding-horizon orchestration with a validated decision count and commit step;
- re-solve the full horizon at every origin while carrying remaining job energy per policy and
  settling only committed intervals;
- implemented an optional joint policy using exact upper-quantile temperature, price, and carbon
  inputs, with incomplete scenarios rejected rather than silently falling back to point forecasts;
- publish rolling mode, decision-level solver records, decision provenance, and remaining-energy
  state through the existing CLI and 12-artifact contract.

### Phase 5 — post-hoc risk validation

- backtest each declared upper quantile against realized values over settled single-window slots or
  committed rolling slots only;
- publish inclusive empirical coverage, signed coverage gap, a 95% Wilson interval, exceedance
  magnitudes, and pinball loss for temperature, price, and carbon intensity;
- expose identical diagnostics in `replay-metrics.json` and the self-contained HTML report;
- preserve the claim boundary: the diagnostics neither recalibrate decisions nor establish joint
  coverage, CVaR, or production performance.

### Phase 6 — multi-scenario robustness

- run two or more complete, hash-bound replay studies without mutating their inputs or provenance;
- enforce one horizon, interval, execution mode, rolling shape, risk-policy availability, policy
  set, and currency before aggregating results;
- publish per-policy feasibility, equal-weight mean and worst signed cost/emissions/peak changes
  from each scenario's ASAP baseline;
- publish a three-objective Pareto frontier restricted to policies feasible in every scenario,
  while preserving every scenario's complete 12-artifact evidence run;
- preserve the claim boundary: equal weights are not probabilities and a finite scenario maximum
  is not a tail-risk guarantee.

No production actuation is introduced by these phases.

## 11. Acceptance gates

v0.2 is not complete until all of the following are true:

1. A fresh checkout can run the offline reference replay with one documented command.
2. Every schedule conserves job energy within a declared numerical tolerance.
3. Capacity, release-time, deadline, and maximum-power violations are reported, never silently
   dropped.
4. The replay uses issued forecasts for decisions and realized values only for settlement.
5. The report includes `kWh`, `kgCO2e`, peak `kW`, currency cost, SLA results, and oracle regret.
6. Data provenance and license attribution are present and machine-readable.
7. Offline tests, strict type checking, lint, documentation build, and package build pass.
8. README claims distinguish measured telemetry, public external data, simulated values, and
   scenario assumptions.
9. Rolling runs preserve per-policy job energy across origins and expose every committed decision.
10. Configured risk quantiles are complete and causally available for all three inputs; missing
    values fail explicitly, and reports do not present the scenario as CVaR, a calibrated
    two-sided interval, or a joint coverage guarantee.
11. Risk-enabled reports validate only settled or committed rows, expose sample count and marginal
    uncertainty, and do not present empirical marginal coverage as a joint guarantee.
12. Suite reports retain every scenario's input/configuration hashes and full replay artifacts;
    infeasible policies remain visible and cannot enter the Pareto frontier.
13. Suite documentation and reports identify equal weighting, feasible-only aggregation, and the
    finite declared worst-case boundary without claiming probability-weighted robustness.

## 12. Migration rule

The legacy shadow scheduler remains supported and continues to produce generic index metrics; it
must not be presented as the v0.2 engineering result. The replay kernel consumes the new contracts
directly without changing the legacy API. Removal or conversion of the legacy workload schema will
require a separately documented deprecation release.
